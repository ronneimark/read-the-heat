  • Workers with cardiovascular disease, obesity, certain medications, or prior heat illness

About Acclimatization

What Is Heat Acclimatization?

Heat acclimatization is the process by which the body progressively adapts to working in hot environments through repeated heat exposures over days to weeks. According to NIOSH (2016), acclimatization produces physiological changes that improve the body's ability to tolerate heat stress — including increased sweating efficiency, more stable cardiovascular function, and lower core body temperature and heart rate at a given workload.

Why It Matters

NIOSH identifies lack of acclimatization as one of the major factors associated with worker heat-related illness and death. Most heat-related fatalities occur during the first few days of working in heat, before the body has adapted (NIOSH, 2016). Acclimatization status is therefore central to selecting the appropriate work-rest schedule.

How Acclimatization Develops

For new workers or those newly assigned to hot environments, NIOSH recommends a graduated exposure schedule beginning at no more than 20% of the usual heat exposure on Day 1, increasing by approximately 20% each day over a 7- to 14-day period (NIOSH, 2016, p. 105). For returning workers who have been away from heat work for 7 or more days, a 4-day return ramp is recommended: 50% → 60% → 80% → 100% of usual exposure (NIOSH, 2016, p. 105).

How Long It Lasts

Acclimatization is not permanent. A worker who has been absent from heat work for 7 or more consecutive days loses a meaningful portion of their acquired adaptation and should be treated as unacclimatized until re-acclimatization is complete. This is why this tool distinguishes between acclimatized workers, new unacclimatized workers, and returning unacclimatized workers.

Source
