Heat Index–Based Work-Rest Schedule

The Heat Index (HI) combines air temperature and relative humidity to estimate perceived temperature. This table synthesizes OSHA heat illness prevention guidance with NIOSH-based work-rest principles. Heat Index is measured in °F (°C equivalents shown). Indoor, no solar load. Standard work clothing assumed. Worker status is set at the top of the page.

Above 54°C		 DANGER! DANGER! DANGER! DANGER!
Imminent danger. All work should cease until conditions are corrected. Evacuation if engineering controls cannot reduce heat to safe levels.
Hydration guidance (consistent with OSHA/NIOSH): Workers should drink approximately 1 cup (8 oz / 240 mL) of cool water every 15–20 minutes during heat-exposed work — not waiting until thirsty. Avoid caffeine and alcohol. Electrolyte replacement is important for prolonged exposures (>2 hours) with heavy sweating.
Key limitation of heat index for occupational use: Heat index was developed for shade conditions with 5 mph wind. In indoor environments with: (a) direct radiant heat from equipment, ovens, or hot surfaces; (b) very high or very low air movement; or (c) PPE/impermeable clothing — heat index significantly underestimates actual heat stress. WBGT is the preferred occupational measure in these settings.
⚠ Note: The unacclimatized work-rest cycles shown here do not capture the full acclimatization process recommended by NIOSH and OSHA. For more detailed guidance on acclimatization protocols, employers should follow the recommendations in the NIOSH Criteria Document.
