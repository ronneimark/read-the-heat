What industry is this work in?

This tool calculates NIOSH-recommended work-rest cycles for indoor workers exposed to heat — in about 30 seconds.

The purpose of this site is to identify the need for breaks from work in hot conditions based on the intensity of the work and the environmental conditions:

  1. Pick your industry — choose the sector that best matches your workplace. ↗ click to see
  2. Describe the work — select the physical activity level (light, moderate, heavy, or very heavy). ↗ click to see
  3. Acclimatization status — indicate whether the worker is new, returning after time off, or fully acclimatized. This affects the safe exposure limits. ↗ click to see
  4. Clothing & PPE — indicate whether the worker is wearing standard clothing or impermeable/vapor-barrier PPE. A +10°C correction is applied to the WBGT for impermeable PPE. ↗ click to see
  5. Measure and enter the heat — use a WBGT meter, a thermometer + humidity reading, or a standard thermometer. The tool recommends work-rest minutes per hour based on what you can measure. ↗ click to see
  6. View recommended work-rest cycle — once all inputs are entered, follow the arrow to see your personalized work-rest schedule. ↗ click to see

  • ⚠️ Thermometers alone are not sufficient. Organizations relying on a plain thermometer during hot conditions should assume 100% relative humidity — the physical maximum, and therefore the most conservative possible heat stress estimate — this tool does that automatically — to ensure workers are fully protected. Organizations with workers exposed to heat should invest in instruments that can actually measure it.
  • 🤝 Workers and their representatives should have transparent and continuous access to those instruments and to the information.

    For more information about the instruments and their functions, see the Instrument step.
  • 👷 Workers — identify the breaks you and your co-workers can push for based on health and safety needs.
  • 🧑‍💼 Supervisors — check conditions before a shift and post the recommended schedule for the crew.
  • 🦺 Safety reps — run through scenarios during a training or walkthrough to show workers what protection looks like in practice.
  • ✊ Union stewards — use this tool to document potential grievances and charges based on the employer's obligation to provide a safe workplace.
  • 📋 Safety committees — document conditions and recommended cycles as part of a heat illness prevention plan.
NOTE: Individual Health Factors play a role in how individuals adapt to heat stress. These formulas do not take that into consideration, but workers and employers may need to. Health factors commonly impacting heat stress can be viewed on this site.
