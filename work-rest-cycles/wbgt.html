Acclimatized worker
  • Has completed a heat acclimatization protocol
  • Has not been absent from heat work for a week or more
  • New workers: 7–14 day graduated ramp (≤20% of usual duration on day 1, +20% per day)
  • Returning workers with prior experience: 4-day ramp (50% → 60% → 80% → 100%)
  • Acclimatization can be regained in 2–3 days upon return to hot work
  • Benefits per NIOSH (2016): increased sweating efficiency, stabilized circulation, lower core temperature and heart rate at the same workload
  • Charts use: NIOSH Recommended Exposure Limits (REL) — Figure 8-2
Unacclimatized worker — select for:
  • New employees who have not yet completed the acclimatization ramp
  • Workers returning after a week or more away from heat exposure
  • Workers newly assigned to hot areas
  • Workers with conditions increasing heat illness risk: cardiovascular disease, obesity, certain medications, prior heat illness
  • Per NIOSH (2016): "lack of acclimatization has been shown to be a major factor associated with worker heat-related illness and death"
  • Charts use: NIOSH Recommended Alert Limits (RAL) — for unacclimatized workers (Figure 8-1), approximately 2–3°C (3–5°F) more protective than REL

NIOSH Recommended Alert Limits (RAL) — Unacclimatized Workers

NIOSH (2016) recommends a progressive daily exposure schedule — gradually increasing the proportion of the workday spent in heat until full acclimatization is achieved. Work-rest cycles appropriate to the activity level and conditions should still be applied during all heat exposure periods.

New Workers

No prior heat exposure — full 5+ day ramp required. Full acclimatization typically takes 7–14 days.

During all acclimatization periods:
  • Consult an occupational health professional regarding appropriate work-rest cycles for the specific activity level and environmental conditions
  • Provide cool water (1 cup / 8 oz every 15–20 minutes) and shaded or air-conditioned rest areas
  • Assign a buddy or supervisor to monitor for early signs of heat illness
  • Workers at higher risk (cardiovascular disease, obesity, certain medications, prior heat illness) may require individualized plans
  • Reduce exposure duration or stop work if heat illness symptoms appear
Environmental monitoring required throughout acclimatization. The schedule above controls when workers are in the heat (% of shift). It does not replace monitoring of what conditions those periods involve. Per NIOSH (2016), unacclimatized workers should not be exposed to combinations of metabolic and environmental heat greater than the Recommended Alert Limits (RAL) — even on day 1 at 20% exposure.

Source: National Institute for Occupational Safety and Health. (2016). NIOSH criteria for a recommended standard: Occupational exposure to heat and hot environments (DHHS [NIOSH] Publication No. 2016-106). U.S. Department of Health and Human Services.
